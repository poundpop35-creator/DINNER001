WRITING GUIDE (applies to every writer and editor):
- Language: Thai, warm and clear like a great tutor talking to a busy working adult; short sentences; define jargon at first use as ไทย (English); concrete Thai-life analogies and examples (office, condo, market, LINE group, traffic) — but facts stay academic-accurate.
- Dense: include the specific content RU textbooks and exams use — names of thinkers and their works with years, classifications (e.g., 'แบ่งเป็น 4 ประเภท ได้แก่ …'), definitions, laws and section numbers when certain, Thai institutional facts, timelines. Prefer lists and tables to make density scannable.
- Engaging: open with a hook (scene/puzzle/surprising fact), build understanding step by step, show why it matters, end with a teaser for the next lesson. Occasional rhetorical questions. No fluff.
- Accuracy over volume: write only what you are confident is correct; verify uncertain facts with WebSearch; when unsure, generalise or omit. Never invent quotes, statistics, page numbers, or URLs.
- Neutrality: Thai political events and institutions described factually and neutrally; the monarchy only in constitutional terms; no partisan opinions.
- Originality: own words; do not copy textbook or exam-sheet text; short attributed quotations from classic works are OK.
- References: real sources only, e.g. primary classics (author, title, year), official legal/international texts (รัฐธรรมนูญแห่งราชอาณาจักรไทย พ.ศ. 2560, พ.ร.บ. …, UN Charter, ASEAN Charter, Vienna Conventions), Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy / Britannica entries, สถาบันพระปกเกล้า (wiki.kpi.ac.th), government sites (ocsc.go.th, krisdika.go.th, mfa.go.th), the RU course textbook. Add a url only if you saw that exact URL in a WebSearch result. Each ref.detail says what the reader finds there.
- Format: plain text only (no HTML, no Markdown symbols like ** or #); no < or > characters; '\n\n' inside body for paragraph breaks; Arabic digits; พ.ศ. for Thai events and ค.ศ. for foreign events (both when helpful).

EXPAND NOTES (depth wanted for foundation lessons 1–5):

Lesson 2: realism (Thucydides Melian dialogue, Machiavelli, Hobbes; Morgenthau 6 principles 1948; Waltz 1979; offensive (Mearsheimer 2001) vs defensive), security dilemma (Herz 1950), balance of power methods. Lesson 3: idealism (Wilson 14 points), liberal institutionalism (Keohane 1984), complex interdependence (Keohane & Nye 1977), democratic peace (Kant 1795, Doyle), constructivism (Wendt 1992/1999), English School briefly. Lesson 5: IPE perspectives, comparative advantage (Ricardo 1817), Bretton Woods (IMF, World Bank; GATT 1947 → WTO 1995), globalization, tragedy of the commons (Hardin 1968), Paris Agreement 2015.

PART 2 LESSON PLAN (lessons 6+; titles may be refined; scope notes are hints you must verify — drop what you cannot confirm):
6. วิวัฒนาการของระบบระหว่างประเทศ: จากเวสต์ฟาเลียถึงโลกหลังสงครามเย็น — Westphalia 1648, Vienna 1815 and Concert of Europe, WWI and Versailles 1919, League of Nations 1920, WWII, Cold War (Truman Doctrine 1947, Marshall Plan, NATO 1949, Warsaw Pact 1955, Cuban Missile Crisis 1962, détente, 1989, 1991), unipolar moment and multipolarity; Thailand briefly.
7. ผลประโยชน์แห่งชาติและนโยบายต่างประเทศ — national interest (Morgenthau), vital vs secondary, determinants, foreign policy process; Allison 1971 three models; Thai foreign policy traditions (bamboo diplomacy as a common description — verify).
8. เครื่องมือในความสัมพันธ์ระหว่างประเทศ: การทูต โฆษณาชวนเชื่อ เศรษฐกิจ และการทหาร — diplomacy types; Vienna Convention on Diplomatic Relations 1961 (immunity, persona non grata); propaganda techniques; economic tools (aid, trade, sanctions, embargo); military tools (deterrence, alliances, arms control); soft/hard/smart power (Nye).
9. ความขัดแย้ง สงคราม และความร่วมมือระหว่างประเทศ — causes of conflict, types (interstate, intrastate, terrorism), peaceful settlement methods (UN Charter Art. 33), cooperation and international regimes (Krasner 1982), regionalism.
10. กฎหมายระหว่างประเทศและองค์การระหว่างประเทศ — sources (ICJ Statute Art. 38), treaties (VCLT 1969, pacta sunt servanda), recognition, jurisdiction, ICJ (Preah Vihear 1962, 2013), ICC (Rome Statute 1998/2002), UNCLOS 1982 (12/24/200 nm); UN 6 principal organs and veto; specialized agencies; ASEAN (1967 Bangkok Declaration, 5 founders, Charter in force 15 Dec 2008, ASEAN Community 2015 three pillars, ASEAN Way), EU briefly.

COURSE BRIEF: POL2107 การเมืองเปรียบเทียบเบื้องต้น (Introduction to Comparative Politics)

WRITING GUIDE (applies to every writer and editor):
- Language: Thai, warm and clear like a great tutor talking to a busy working adult; short sentences; define jargon at first use as ไทย (English); concrete Thai-life analogies and examples (office, condo, market, LINE group, traffic) — but facts stay academic-accurate.
- Dense: include the specific content RU textbooks and exams use — names of thinkers and their works with years, classifications (e.g., 'แบ่งเป็น 4 ประเภท ได้แก่ …'), definitions, laws and section numbers when certain, Thai institutional facts, timelines. Prefer lists and tables to make density scannable.
- Engaging: open with a hook (scene/puzzle/surprising fact), build understanding step by step, show why it matters, end with a teaser for the next lesson. Occasional rhetorical questions. No fluff.
- Accuracy over volume: write only what you are confident is correct; verify uncertain facts with WebSearch; when unsure, generalise or omit. Never invent quotes, statistics, page numbers, or URLs.
- Neutrality: Thai political events and institutions described factually and neutrally; the monarchy only in constitutional terms; no partisan opinions.
- Originality: own words; do not copy textbook or exam-sheet text; short attributed quotations from classic works are OK.
- References: real sources only, e.g. primary classics (author, title, year), official legal/international texts (รัฐธรรมนูญแห่งราชอาณาจักรไทย พ.ศ. 2560, พ.ร.บ. …, UN Charter, ASEAN Charter, Vienna Conventions), Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy / Britannica entries, สถาบันพระปกเกล้า (wiki.kpi.ac.th), government sites (ocsc.go.th, krisdika.go.th, mfa.go.th), the RU course textbook. Add a url only if you saw that exact URL in a WebSearch result. Each ref.detail says what the reader finds there.
- Format: plain text only (no HTML, no Markdown symbols like ** or #); no < or > characters; '\n\n' inside body for paragraph breaks; Arabic digits; พ.ศ. for Thai events and ค.ศ. for foreign events (both when helpful).

EXPAND NOTES (depth wanted for foundation lessons 1–5):
Lesson 1: why compare (Aristotle’s 158 constitutions), comparative method (Lijphart 1971), units/levels of analysis, conceptual stretching (Sartori 1970). Lesson 2: parliamentary vs presidential vs semi-presidential table (UK, US, France), no-confidence, constructive vote of no confidence (Germany), dual legitimacy, Linz. Lesson 3: unitary, federal, confederal; devolution (UK); decentralization types (political, administrative, fiscal); Thai mapping. Lesson 4: democracy definitions (Schumpeter, Dahl polyarchy 8 guarantees / 2 dimensions), transitions (Huntington waves, O’Donnell & Schmitter), consolidation (Linz & Stepan), hybrid regimes. Lesson 5: Mill’s methods, MSSD/MDSD (Przeworski & Teune 1970), variables, causality, selection bias.

PART 2 LESSON PLAN (lessons 6+; titles may be refined; scope notes are hints you must verify — drop what you cannot confirm):
6. พัฒนาการและแนวการศึกษาการเมืองเปรียบเทียบ — traditional legal-institutional approach, behavioral revolution, systems theory (Easton), structural-functionalism (Almond input/output functions), political economy, new institutionalism (historical, rational choice, sociological) with comparison table.
7. รัฐ ชาติ และสมรรถนะของรัฐ — Weber’s definition; elements of the state; state formation (Tilly), nation and nationalism (Anderson 1983), nation-state, strong/weak/failed states, state capacity and autonomy.
8. วัฒนธรรมการเมืองและการกล่อมเกลาทางการเมือง — Almond & Verba The Civic Culture 1963 (5 countries; parochial/subject/participant; civic culture), agents of socialization, Inglehart post-materialism, Putnam social capital (1993), critiques; neutral Thai examples.
9. การพัฒนาการเมือง ภาวะทันสมัย และทฤษฎีการพึ่งพา — modernization (Lipset 1959, Rostow 1960), political development (Pye: equality, capacity, differentiation), Huntington 1968 (institutionalization vs participation, praetorianism), dependency (Frank, Cardoso & Faletto), world-systems (Wallerstein), bureaucratic authoritarianism (O’Donnell 1973).
10. ระบอบการเมืองแบบต่าง ๆ และรูปแบบประชาธิปไตย — regime typology: democracy, hybrid/competitive authoritarian (Levitsky & Way), authoritarian subtypes (military, single-party, personalist — Geddes 1999), totalitarianism; Lijphart majoritarian vs consensus (two dimensions); electoral and party systems link.

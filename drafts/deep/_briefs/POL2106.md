COURSE BRIEF: POL2106 ปรัชญาการเมืองเบื้องต้น (Introduction to Political Philosophy)

WRITING GUIDE (applies to every writer and editor):
- Language: Thai, warm and clear like a great tutor talking to a busy working adult; short sentences; define jargon at first use as ไทย (English); concrete Thai-life analogies and examples (office, condo, market, LINE group, traffic) — but facts stay academic-accurate.
- Dense: include the specific content RU textbooks and exams use — names of thinkers and their works with years, classifications (e.g., 'แบ่งเป็น 4 ประเภท ได้แก่ …'), definitions, laws and section numbers when certain, Thai institutional facts, timelines. Prefer lists and tables to make density scannable.
- Engaging: open with a hook (scene/puzzle/surprising fact), build understanding step by step, show why it matters, end with a teaser for the next lesson. Occasional rhetorical questions. No fluff.
- Accuracy over volume: write only what you are confident is correct; verify uncertain facts with WebSearch; when unsure, generalise or omit. Never invent quotes, statistics, page numbers, or URLs.
- Neutrality: Thai political events and institutions described factually and neutrally; the monarchy only in constitutional terms; no partisan opinions.
- Originality: own words; do not copy textbook or exam-sheet text; short attributed quotations from classic works are OK.
- References: real sources only, e.g. primary classics (author, title, year), official legal/international texts (รัฐธรรมนูญแห่งราชอาณาจักรไทย พ.ศ. 2560, พ.ร.บ. …, UN Charter, ASEAN Charter, Vienna Conventions), Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy / Britannica entries, สถาบันพระปกเกล้า (wiki.kpi.ac.th), government sites (ocsc.go.th, krisdika.go.th, mfa.go.th), the RU course textbook. Add a url only if you saw that exact URL in a WebSearch result. Each ref.detail says what the reader finds there.
- Format: plain text only (no HTML, no Markdown symbols like ** or #); no < or > characters; '\n\n' inside body for paragraph breaks; Arabic digits; พ.ศ. for Thai events and ค.ศ. for foreign events (both when helpful).

EXPAND NOTES (depth wanted for foundation lessons 1–5):
Lesson 1: normative vs empirical, branches, method of argument, history periods, the textbook’s guiding questions. Lesson 2: Plato (Republic: 3 classes, 4 virtues, cave, decline of regimes) and Aristotle (Politics: polis, 6 forms table, polity, middle class, citizen). Lesson 3: Hobbes/Locke/Rousseau comparison table; Leviathan 1651, Two Treatises 1689, Social Contract 1762, Discourse on Inequality 1755. Lesson 4: Mill On Liberty 1859 (harm principle, domains of liberty, tyranny of the majority, free speech arguments), Berlin 1958, Bentham. Lesson 5: Rawls (original position, 2 principles, lexical priority, maximin), Marx (historical materialism, base/superstructure, surplus value, alienation, class struggle, Manifesto 1848, Capital 1867).

PART 2 LESSON PLAN (lessons 6+; titles may be refined; scope notes are hints you must verify — drop what you cannot confirm):
6. ทำไมเราต้องเชื่อฟังกฎหมาย: พันธะทางการเมืองและการดื้อแพ่ง — Socrates (Apology, Crito), consent (Locke express/tacit) and Hume’s critique, fair play (Hart, Rawls), utilitarian answers, natural law (Aquinas), civil disobedience (Thoreau 1849, Gandhi, King 1963, Rawls’ definition), philosophical anarchism.
7. ใครควรเป็นผู้ปกครอง: จากเพลโตถึงประชาธิปไตยสมัยใหม่ — epistocracy (Plato), mixed constitution (Aristotle, Polybius), divine right vs popular sovereignty, Machiavelli’s realism, participatory (Rousseau), representative (Mill 1861), elite competition (Schumpeter 1942), deliberative (Habermas) briefly; tyranny of the majority (Tocqueville).
8. ปรัชญาการเมืองยุคกลางและยุคฟื้นฟูศิลปวิทยา — Augustine City of God, Aquinas four laws, church–state relations, Machiavelli The Prince (virtù, fortuna, fox and lion, feared vs loved), Bodin sovereignty (1576), Reformation (Luther) briefly.
9. อุดมการณ์การเมืองสมัยใหม่ — liberalism (classical vs social; Locke, Smith, Mill, Green), conservatism (Burke 1790), socialism/communism (utopian vs scientific; social democracy), anarchism (Proudhon, Bakunin, Kropotkin), fascism and Nazism, feminism (Wollstonecraft 1792, de Beauvoir 1949), nationalism; comparison table.
10. การกระจายทรัพย์สินและความยุติธรรมทางเศรษฐกิจ — Locke’s labour theory and provisos, Rousseau on inequality, utilitarian distribution, Marx (exploitation; from each according to ability), Rawls vs Nozick (entitlement theory; Wilt Chamberlain; minimal state 1974), Sen/Nussbaum capability approach briefly, luck egalitarianism briefly.
